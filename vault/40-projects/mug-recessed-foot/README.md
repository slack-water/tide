## Goal

My existing handled two-part mug, with a recessed foot, cast and fired from a three-part plaster mold.

## Scope

**In:** a base piece that forms the recessed foot; registration between the base and both side halves; a printed mother mold (or cottle) for the base; casting, trimming, firing and glazing the result.
**Out:** double-walled or rotocast vessels, full-cast solids, new mesh tooling (plain CAD unless blocked), changes to the mug's shape or handle. These are in the inbox, not folded in.

## Done looks like

One mug, cast from the three-part mold, fired, with a clean foot ring and no cracks at the foot. Stretch: three casts of the same mug with matching foot geometry, as a first check on repeatability.

## Risks to watch

- **Registration:** base must seat on the side halves and still let them part sideways, so it needs keys or a ledge in addition to the existing half-to-half key.
- **Foot ring:** a thin plaster ridge and the most fragile part of the mold. Generous draft, soft profile.
- **Cast thickness:** drain-cast bottoms run thick, and an abrupt thick-to-thin change at the ring is a crack point. Trim the bottom at leather-hard and dry slowly.
- **Demolding order:** work out whether the base releases before or after the sides.
- Scope creep: if more than three next actions are active at once, cut back to the smallest version.
