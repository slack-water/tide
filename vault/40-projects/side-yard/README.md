# side yard

## goal

a side yard that drains, takes almost no upkeep, gives the pup a covered potty spot, and gives the dining room doors something worth looking at.

anything that doesn't serve that sentence goes to the inbox, not into this plan (see [[FAILURE-MODES]] → scope creep. "i'm kind of extra" is the early warning).

## done looks like

- no standing water 24 hours after a 1" rain, and none touching the foundation at any time
- the pup goes out the dog door onto a covered potty patch that rinses clean and doesn't smell
- one deliberate thing to look at from the dining table
- upkeep is a leaf blower and a hose, not weeding

phases 1–3 are the project. phase 4+ is the fun part, and it only starts once 1–3 are done.

## what the site actually is

**orientation.** the front faces 120° (ESE), so the yard runs ESE (block wall, front) to WNW (back patio). the house is on the SSW side of the yard. **the yard sits on the north side of a two-story house.**

**sun, roughly** (latitude ~33.5°, ~24 ft eave):

| season | noon sun altitude | house shadow reaches | what the yard gets |
|---|---|---|---|
| dec | ~33° | ~37 ft, past the neighbor's house | full shade all day |
| mar / sep | ~56° | ~16 ft, over the fence | shade, except early/late light down the length |
| jun | ~80° | ~4 ft | the fence side gets a few hours. low morning and evening sun runs down the long axis |

so: **plan for shade.** the fence side gets the most light. fruiting espalier probably won't fruit here. check before buying plants (phase 0).

**zones** (back to front):

- **utility (≈ first 14 ft).** AC condenser, deck box, irrigation manifold, the little iron gate. flagstone path today.
- **the doors (≈ middle 12 ft).** double doors and the dog door, both on the house side and facing straight across ~9 ft to the vinyl fence. this is where water pools, against the foundation.
- **the front end (≈ last 14 ft).** square-paver patio, block wall, the cinder-block shelf, the grooming table.

**the key design fact:** the dining room view is **across** the yard, not down it. what you see from the table is ~9 ft of ground and then a white vinyl fence with the neighbor's stucco wall above it. the fence panel opposite the doors is the stage. that's where the effort pays off most.

**things i noticed in the photos:**

- in the door photos, soil and turf sit right up against the stucco with no visible weep screed. code wants 4" of clearance above soil (2" above paving). the regrade has to fix this, not just the puddle
- the pooled water sits *between* the doors and the turf, so the door zone is a low point
- loose irrigation tubing and conduit are running at the surface by the doors
- the neighbor's hedge over the block wall looks like **oleander**, and the potted **pencil cactus / firesticks** are euphorbia. both are toxic to dogs. the pup will be pottying right next to the oleander leaf drop
- there's a GFCI outlet by the doors. useful for a pump, lighting, or a fountain

## phase 0 — diagnose (1–2 weekends, ~$0–50)

*premature action on discomfort applies here: the puddle is annoying, but digging before you know why is how you build the wrong drain.*

- [ ] **find where the water comes from.** in the next rain, go out and watch. downspouts (where do the side-yard downspouts discharge?), irrigation overspray or a leaking valve at the manifold, AC condensate, runoff off the patio slab, runoff from the neighbor's side. if it's mostly irrigation, the fix is half as big
- [ ] **map the grade.** string line and line level, or a cheap laser, every 5 ft along the center and at the doors. find the real low point and how much fall exists front to back. → [grade map](#grade-map) below (3 lines × 9 stations, rotary laser)
- [ ] **perc test, 2 holes** (one middle, one front end). dig 8" wide × 24" deep, fill and let drain overnight to presoak, refill, measure the drop per hour. **this result picks the drainage strategy** (see below)
- [ ] **DigAlert (811).** free, and required in CA before you excavate. side yards are where gas and electric run
- [ ] **HOA check.** awning, anything taller than the fence, and changes to the shared fence
- [ ] **sun check.** photos from the dining table at 9a / noon / 3p / 6p on one day now, and again in spring

### grade map

**tool.** a self-leveling rotary laser on a tripod, with a receiver on a direct-read grade rod. it's one person: set the tripod once, walk the rod to each stake, read where it beeps. keep it out for phase 1 and check depth as you dig.

**datum (0).** top of the patio slab at the side-yard edge, marked with paint. ______ (write where exactly)

**zeroing the rod.** stand the rod on the datum, then slide the tape until the receiver beeps at 0. after that every reading is **inches below datum** (positive = lower). check the zero on the datum at the start of each session and any time the tripod gets bumped.

**the grid.** stations every 5 ft from the patio edge (0) to the block wall (40; the wall is at ~41). three lines:

- **house (H):** 1 ft off the stucco
- **center (C):** ~4.9 ft off the stucco, which is the trench line
- **fence (F):** 1 ft off the vinyl

also read the **bottom of the stucco / weep screed** at each house stake (S). that's what the 4" clearance is measured from.

#### 2 — target grade

the assumptions (change them here if they change):

- the **outlet is at sta 0** (patio end: sump / pump line under the slab). if the perc test or the map puts the outlet at the front, flip the offset column so sta 40 gets 0
- the trench line falls **1%** toward the outlet: ⅝" per 5 ft
- the house and fence lines sit **1" above center**: 2% over the ~3.9 ft to the trench
- finish grade at the house is **at least 4" below the stucco bottom**
- the gravel section is **6"** (2" finish over 4" base), so dig to finish + 6"
- **under the concrete pavers** (landing, nook), the section is about **8½"**: ~2⅜" paver + 1" bedding + 5" base. dig to finish + 8½" there, and check against the actual paver thickness once it's picked

**the one number to work out: C₀**, the finish depth of the trench line at the outlet. for each station, take `S + 5 + offset`, then use the **largest** result. (+4" is the clearance, +1" is the cross slope; the offset carries the 1% back to the outlet.) that one station controls the whole profile.

C₀ = ______

| sta (ft) | offset (1%) | C finish = C₀ − offset | H & F finish = C finish − 1 | H cut | C cut | F cut |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0 | | | | | |
| 5 | ⅝ | | | | | |
| 10 | 1¼ | | | | | |
| 15 | 1¾ | | | | | |
| 20 | 2⅜ | | | | | |
| 25 | 3 | | | | | |
| 30 | 3⅝ | | | | | |
| 35 | 4¼ | | | | | |
| 40 | 4¾ | | | | | |

**cut** = (finish + 6) − existing. positive means dig that many inches. negative means fill. write it on the stake as `cut 3` or `fill 1` (not C/F, which are the line names).

**checks before digging:**

- [ ] house finish is ≥ 4" below S at every station (it will be, if C₀ was the largest result)
- [ ] the paver tops at the door landing are ¾–1" below the threshold and pitched 2% away from the house
- [ ] if cuts at the house run past ~8", look again before digging. you may be near the footing. a steeper trench or a lower outlet might be better than a deeper cut
- [ ] the C finish has no station higher than the one downstream of it (no bellies)
- [ ] the trench bottom (C finish + 24") sits above the outlet / sump inlet

tolerance: ±¼" for the gravel finish and base. ±½" for the dirt under the gravel. **under the pavers, tighter:** base ±¼", screeded bedding within ⅛" under a 6 ft straightedge, ≤ ⅛" lippage between pavers (large-format units rock on any hump). when you're unsure, err toward more fall away from the house.

### the drainage decision

a 9.75 ft yard can't meet the usual dry-well setbacks (~10 ft from foundations, ~5 ft from property lines). in SoCal tract fill, which is often expansive clay, pushing water into the ground 5 ft from a slab can heave it. so the perc test picks the path:

| perc result | strategy |
|---|---|
| **> ~1"/hr** (drains well) | **shallow infiltration trench** instead of a deep point dry well. perforated pipe in a ~18" wide × 24" deep washed-gravel trench wrapped in fabric, down the center line, so it stays as far from both foundations as the yard allows. the trench *is* the storage: 30 ft × 1.5 × 2 ft ≈ 90 cu ft of gravel ≈ 250 gal of void, about 1" of rain on 400 sq ft. add a small flo-well at the low end only if the downspouts also discharge here |
| **< ~0.5"/hr** (clay) | **sump + pump.** "can't tie in" is a gravity problem, and a pump makes it go away. french drain → buried sump basin at the low point → small float-switch pump (on the GFCI by the doors) → 1.25–1.5" discharge line pushed under or run along the patio slab to the existing curb-drain system |
| in between | trench first, sized to the perc. rough in the sump basin and conduit so a pump can drop in later without re-digging |

either way, the french drain itself is the same: surface sloped away from the house at 2% (¼" per ft) for at least 3–4 ft, toward the center-line trench, with ~1% fall along the trench to the outlet.

**the rock total is about 10½ cy (~15 tons)**: 9 cy of ¾" clean, ¾ cy of chip, 1¼ cy of field gravel, plus the pebbles.

**spoil going out is roughly 12–15 cy loose**: the trench (~3.3 cy in the ground) plus 3–8½" of surface sections over ~400 sf, swelled about 25% once dug. the grade map will move this number, because the cut needed for stucco clearance either is that section depth or adds to it. this is the biggest solo-labor line in the project: at 4–5 cf per wheelbarrow, it's 60–80 trips in, and about as many out. get a dumpster or a dump-trailer haul-off, and have the rock dumped as close to the gate as the driver will go.

**pad sizing.** the plan's 2 × 4 ft pads become two 24 × 24s each. with 24 × 36, the pads shrink to 2 × 3 ft and the paver area drops to ~159 sf.

**not included:** arbor footings (the fabricator specs them), the AC screen pier blocks, the conduit and drip sleeves, a sump basin and pump (only if the perc test calls for it), joint material for the paver joints, and the plaster skim.

## phase 1 — drainage and grade (the actual problem)

- [ ] clear everything: flagstone (keep it for reuse), square pavers (keep), turf, the cinder-block shelf, loose tubing
- [ ] lower the grade along the house to expose 4" of stucco above soil
- [ ] dig the trench, lay fabric, gravel, and pipe (+ sump/pump or flo-well per phase 0)
- [ ] **while it's open, lay sleeves.** an empty 1" conduit for low-voltage lighting and fountain/pump power, plus a sleeve under the path for drip lines. a few dollars now saves digging it all up again later
- [ ] **convert the irrigation zone to drip** at the manifold (filter + pressure regulator + ½" mainline). bury the lines in the sleeves. no more spray in this yard
- [ ] backfill and compact. **test with a hose before any finish surface goes down**

## phase 2 — surfaces (after this, the yard counts as done)

one permeable base everywhere: 3–4" compacted class II base or ¾" crushed rock, with a ~2" finish layer.

- **door landing.** a level, solid pad the width of the doors, ~4 ft deep, ¾–1" below the threshold, pitched away. options: reset the square pavers on screed, or a pour-in-place concrete pad you make yourself. replaces the tile/mat/paver patchwork
- **path.** reset the flagstone as stepping stones in ¼–⅜" gravel. you already own it
- **pup potty (front end, just past the dog door).** ~4 × 6 ft. in a shade yard, regular turf never dries and starts to stink. use either **K9 turf with a fully permeable backing + zeolite infill** (Zeofill) over the base, sitting directly over the drain, or **pea gravel** in a steel- or timber-edged bed. both get hosed down into the drain. needs a hose bib within reach
- **everything else.** gravel. stabilized DG is easier to blow leaves off, but it drains slower; plain gravel is the forgiving choice here
- **edging.** steel or aluminum edging between gravel, potty, and beds so nothing migrates

## phase 3 — shelter

- [ ] a lean-to awning over the doors and dog door, extending over the potty patch. ~5 ft projection, ledger on the house, posts on the fence side or cantilevered brackets. polycarbonate keeps the dining room bright; slats + polycarb is warmer looking. gutter it into the drain
- the rear patio already has an aluminum cover. matching it is the low-effort option. a timber one you build is the "extra" option, and fine here because it's real shelter, not decoration

## phase 4 — the view (options; pick one centerpiece)

the stage is the fence panel opposite the doors, seen from the table at ~9–12 ft. **one strong thing, not five medium ones.**

because the fence is vinyl (probably shared, and it can't carry weight), build a **freestanding frame**: two 4×4 or steel posts in footings 6–8" in front of the fence, with a trellis or panel between them. that's the backbone for every option below.

| option | what it is | sun need | effort | values hit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. espalier camellia** | *camellia sasanqua* trained flat on wires in the frame. evergreen, winter flowers (exactly when the yard is fully shaded and you're looking at it most), dog-safe | shade ok | low build, years of patient pruning | soul, growth (new craft) |
| **B. raised planter + ceramic face** | a long, low (~18–24") planter along the fence, faced with your own tile: relief, glaze tests, a pattern. shade plants in it | shade ok | big ceramics project | soul, growth, making |
| **C. ceramic wall fountain** | a thrown or slab-built basin + spout on the frame, recirculating, powered by the sleeve from phase 1. you hear it from the table with the doors open | none | medium. needs pump, reservoir, splash control | soul, vitality (sound, water) |
| **D. screen + pots** | slatted cedar or cor-ten screen hides the fence, with 3 large handmade pots in front (japanese maple, ferns, cast-iron plant) | shade ok | moderate | soul, making |

**my recommendation: A + a little of D now, then C or B as a later making project.** the camellia espalier on a cedar-slat screen fixes the "staring at vinyl" problem, flowers in the shade season, and suits a shade yard. leave a gap in the frame where a ceramic fountain or tile panel can slot in later, so the "extra" has a planned home instead of growing sideways.

espalier fruit trees want 6+ hrs of sun; this yard won't give that except maybe on the fence side in summer. if you want to try anyway, a dwarf citrus or fig in a pot at the front block wall (which gets some afternoon sun in summer) is the low-risk experiment.

### shade + dog-safe plant shortlist (verify each on ASPCA before buying)

camellia sasanqua / japonica · cast-iron plant (*aspidistra*) · boston or holly fern (not asparagus fern, which is toxic) · heuchera · liriope · bamboo palm (*chamaedorea*) · japanese maple in a big pot

**avoid/remove:** sago palm, pencil cactus/euphorbias, podocarpus, clivia. keep an eye on oleander leaf drop over the block wall.

## values check

- **vitality (5).** this is a body project: digging, hauling, building. being outside doing it is a big part of why it's worth doing yourself
- **soul (4).** the view from the dining doors is the soul piece. it's why phase 4 is in the plan at all
- **growth (3).** drainage, concrete, espalier, and an outdoor-rated ceramic piece are all new skills that build on each other
- **conflict.** growth + soul vs. scope creep. the guardrail: phases 1–3 finish before any ceramics for this yard start. pick one phase-4 centerpiece
